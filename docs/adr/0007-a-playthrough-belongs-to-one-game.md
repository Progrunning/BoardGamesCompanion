# A playthrough belongs to one game, and expansions in play are descriptive

Players want to record which expansions were on the table ([#102](https://github.com/Progrunning/BoardGamesCompanion/issues/102)). We record them as **expansions in play** — a set of expansion ids on the playthrough of the **base game** — and give them no weight in any count or statistic. A base-plus-expansion play is a play of the base game and of nothing else: it produces no **recorded result** for the expansion, adds nothing to the expansion's play count, and the expansion's own plays never merge upward into the base game's history. The feature records a fact about a play; it does not create a second play.

## Status

accepted

## Considered options

- **Count the play under both the base game and each expansion** (rejected: a **recorded result** is a player's outcome *in a play*, so one evening of Gloomhaven with two expansions would yield three results per player. Every statistic that walks results — **competitive win rate**, **head-to-head record** and therefore **rival** and **nemesis** — silently triples the weight of plays by people who own expansions, and the numbers stop being comparable between players, which is the property ADR-0004 exists to protect. "How often do I bring this expansion out?" is answerable by filtering base-game plays, with no second play needed).
- **Count plays but not scores** (rejected: it reads as the safe middle and is the worst of the three — the play count and the win rate on an expansion's statistics page would then be derived from different sets of plays, so the page contradicts itself and no reader can tell which number to trust).
- **Model base and expansions as peer games on one play, with one marked primary** (rejected: dissolves the base-game/expansion distinction BGG itself maintains, and every consumer of `Playthrough.boardGameId` becomes a list walk guarding against the primary being absent — for no behaviour a single id plus a descriptive set does not already give).
- **Several linked playthroughs, one per game, joined by a group id** (rejected: multiplies rows, invents a second kind of play that can be half-deleted or half-edited, and leaves every existing query to learn about grouping).
- **Migrate existing expansion-logged plays onto their base games automatically** (rejected: it rewrites **user-authored data**, the only data in the app whose loss is permanent, on a guess about intent. Three cases defeat it — BGG flags standalone-playable expansions such as *Dominion: Intrigue*, where the existing play is already correct; expansions listing several base games have no single destination; and the base game may not be in the collection at all, since the link lives in the *base game's* `expansions` list, so the app would have to invent a **collection entry** for a game the user does not own).

## Consequences

- **Logging a play on an expansion stays possible.** New plays are soft-closed, not closed: tapping Log Play on an expansion offers the base game with that expansion pre-ticked, and keeps a "log here anyway" escape for standalone-playable expansions. `CONTEXT.md` says a playthrough belongs to exactly one game *normally* a base game, deliberately — the glossary describes what is true rather than what is tidy.
- **Legacy plays stay where they are, and move only by hand.** Plays already logged against an expansion remain valid and visible on that expansion. A per-play action, offered from the expansion's plays history when the base game is in the collection, reassigns one play's `boardGameId` and adds the expansion as an expansion in play. Bulk move is deliberately absent: a one-tap rewrite of many plays with no undo is a different risk class, and the user doing it a twelfth time is the evidence that it is wanted.
- **Expansions in play are stored as ids only** — `@HiveField(10) List<String>? expansionIdsInPlay` on `Playthrough`, shipping on Hive rather than waiting for the SQLite schema of ADR-0003, which absorbs it as a join table like every other nested list. Names are resolved from the base game's `expansions` list at read time; storing them alongside would create a second copy of **catalogue data** that goes stale when BGG renames an expansion.
- **The picker offers every expansion BGG lists for the game, not only owned ones**, in-collection first. The list is already local, so this costs no request, and "we played with a friend's copy" is otherwise unloggable. Expansions in play are editable after the fact on the edit page, like scores and notes — a play started "now" is logged before the game is over.
- **Nothing reaches the web.** Expansions in play are absent from the **statistics snapshot** sent with a **published collection**; since each publish replaces the snapshot wholesale, adding them later is additive and needs no migration — which is the argument for not doing it now.
- **Statistics are untouched by this change.** No filter by expansion, no most-played-expansions block. The concern that expansions inflate scores is answered by showing the expansions next to the score on the play itself, not by a new number.
